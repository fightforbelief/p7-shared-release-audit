# Analysis specification, 23 November 2026

Written before inspecting IPEDS values or running the solver. This is a timestamped development specification, not a registered confirmatory study.

## Question and estimand

For an anchor application mix b, replace a share rho with any mix of the same institutions, preserving both within-site group proportions and outcome rates. Compute the attainable worst upward and downward change in the women-minus-men admission-rate gap relative to the same anchor and same latent table. Evaluate exact counts and rectangular count uncertainty. Compare against allowing anchor and target to use different tables, against one-site replacements, and against ignoring release uncertainty. No classifier training or causal inference is involved.

## Data and cases

Use NCES IPEDS fall 2022 and 2023 admissions files. Prefer revised CSV within the official ZIP. Retain reported (R) applications and admissions for both women and men, strictly positive application denominators and valid integer admission counts. Denominators refer only to these two published categories. Sort eligible units into three bins by combined application volume: [100,1000), [1000,10000), [10000,infinity). Within each bin sort by SHA-256 of the ASCII string 'P7:' followed by UNITID. Take the first 80 institutions or all if fewer; partition sequentially into panels of eight, omit incomplete panels. These synthetic portfolios test a finite-table diagnostic. Neither institutions nor panels are sampled for population inference. Repeat the same procedure in each year; cross-year overlap is expected.

## Design

Use observed application shares for the anchor. Primary profile rho = 0.10, with 0, 0.01, 0.25 and 1 as boundary/sensitivity checks. Use an exact-count control and a hypothetical release rounding admitted counts to the nearest multiple of 10 or 100 (half ties upward, intersect with feasible integers). Applications stay public. Interval endpoints quantify ambiguity conditional on the release. Rounding has no differential privacy guarantee.

Also emulate a public-denominator outcome-private Laplace release at epsilon 1 on the first panel in each size bin and year. Use 100 independent seeded public-data simulations per panel at rho=0.10. Compare an honest simultaneous 95% noise-inversion box with clipped plug-in counts and independently ranged anchor/target. Report frequencies against exact-table extrema, uncertainty width and Monte Carlo precision. The seeded simulation is not a production privacy mechanism.

Report panel medians, ranges or frequencies with explicit denominators. Include an independent exhaustive rate-corner and grid/analytic mixture oracle on small fixtures, interior and degenerate examples and attainability checks. Disclose failed comparisons, differences from this specification and selection limits.

## Exploratory additions

Fix the 2022 panels, retain complete panels with valid 2023 records for every member, and add the nine University of California undergraduate campuses as a named system. Compare the observed 2023 change with the 2022 frozen-site projection at the observed 2023 weights and with its envelope at the smallest admissible replacement fraction. Neither the temporal replay nor the named case is confirmatory. Cross-year results may reflect changes in group shares, outcomes or reporting. The UC case uses application counts per campus.

The small primary-radius error of single-site replacement suggested a second exploratory analysis. We derived a uniform curvature allowance for the single-site audit and evaluated its enclosure and excess width on all existing profiles. ## External release validation (before examining California effects)

Use the US Department of Education's 2018–19 EDFacts school mathematics proficiency public-use release (snapshot 13AUG20, archival mirror). This is the final pre-pandemic year. Select California, all grades (00), the ALL and LEP rows, with tested counts as denominators. The focal contrast is students classified as English learners minus all remaining tested students. Use the published school-wide proficiency interval and the learner interval. The remaining group is their complement; its independent marginal bounds are derived by projection. The target information set is these two all-grade rows, not auxiliary grades, race categories, district-level releases or linked records. Retain explicit suppressed percentages as unrestricted rates when denominators are available. Reject missing/nonpositive group denominators and infeasible intervals, documenting all selection losses. No artificial rounding of these public values.

Use every district with 3–25 eligible schools and complete coverage of its schools with an ALL all-grade row. The cap bounds the exact pair search and defines the population studied. Compare the full joint polygon with the *exact* solver on its projected marginal rectangle on identical inputs. Primary: 10% unrestricted replacement and a ±5 percentage-point (pp) certificate. Report both endpoints, the maximum absolute-change radius, the number of flips, and the full interval of tolerances at which the two decisions differ. A radius reduction of at least 0.1 pp is the prespecified size criterion; report separately whether it coincides with a threshold flip. Sensitivities: replacement of 25% and 100%, and tolerances of 2.5 and 7.5 pp. Report an additional fixed composition, giving each school equal weight instead of each tested student equal weight. This is a separate policy contrast, not a realized future shift.

Decode labels as bins on the nearest integer percentage (include both half-point boundaries for unspecified ties); blank or missing rows are not zero. For the primary radius and fixed mix also check a conservative encoding admitting both nearest-percent and truncated-percent interpretations. Preserve raw labels and report whether they contain any information beyond projections. The solver is sharp relative to the explicit integer count sets. If joint optimization adds little, make shared-release coupling and the linear screening guarantee the central contributions. Report all comparisons even if no decision changes.

After seeing the five-point counts, but before inspecting district endpoints, we added an exploratory decision for the fixed equal-school contrast: can the release establish a strictly positive or strictly negative change? Zero is the natural directional boundary. This question is inapplicable to the unrestricted family, which always includes the unchanged mix. District-specific switching intervals are descriptive, not prospective thresholds.

To assess the narrower submission claim after the external joint-versus-projection results, compare the shared-release exact audit with separate-table subtraction and the existing linear-time inner/outer scan on the same nearest-percent primary district cases. A scan resolves a district when its outer radius is at most 5 pp or its attainable inner radius exceeds 5 pp. Keep these explanatory comparisons distinct from the initial external-study design.

## Novelty check against shared-table baselines

This comparison was designed after the original 387/417 result was known, before computing the new baselines. Keep the full external cohort, both encodings, the previously evaluated variable-weight radii and all three tolerances. Give every method the same attainable single-school witnesses. Use the full joint endpoints to check every claim and projected exact endpoints to check every outer bound. Record paired district decisions, including wins in each direction, with the original scan as reference. Count certifications, violations and abstentions separately. Use no outcome-tuned parameters.

Compare three linear-time specializations of classical bounds: sum the separately optimized single-group changes, transfer both groups to a common reference distribution using the sharp Hilbert-to-total-variation inequality, and bound a reciprocal denominator ratio at its two extrema after expressing both terms under one group's weights. Also combine their valid outer endpoints. As a higher-cost common-mixture comparison, clear both denominators and range the degree-two rational Bernstein coefficients on the simplex over the shared projected table. These are adaptations of published principles to this audit. None uses the exact district output as input to its bound. Report realized standalone and screen-then-exact costs on the 417 primary inputs with common parsing and projection excluded; timings are descriptive. Interpret the incremental decisions and runtime against same-information competitors rather than the raw first-pass rate.
