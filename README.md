# How far can a group rate gap move?

Yunjie Guo and Hanqing Yang · Independent Researcher, Suzhou, China

[Read the manuscript (PDF)](paper/manuscript.pdf) · [LaTeX source](paper/manuscript.tex) · [Study design and amendments](DESIGN.md) · [Windows verification](results/verification.txt)

A consortium may know the number of applications by site and group while having access only to rounded or privacy-perturbed offer counts. How far can the difference in group offer rates move if a fraction of the applications is reassigned to a different mix of the same sites? This audit finds the largest feasible increase and decrease, reusing one latent outcome table for both mixes. It returns the receiving sites and weights that attain each endpoint.

## Contributions and submission scope

* An exact quadratic-time solver reduces a joint search over site mixes and rate tables to recipient pairs with at most five intervals per pair. The fixed-table two-ratio edge property is classical. The common-cell sign rule makes the search over uncertain tables tractable.
* A linear-time scan provides both inner and outer endpoints. We bound the gap between them and construct a three-site instance showing that the quadratic order of the single-site approximation error at small replacement shares is necessary.
* The empirical study covers 60 eight-institution panels, rounding and outcome-noise experiments, a replay across years and the nine undergraduate University of California campuses. The claim is methodological. The panels do not estimate a nationwide discrimination effect or establish that real admissions are generated by fixed site laws.

At 10% replacement with admissions rounded to tens, the median range for small institutions is 7.23 percentage points with a shared table and 21.82 points with separate tables. Across the 24 complete next-year panels, five observed changes exceed the frozen-site exact-count range. The analysis supports submission as a finite-table sensitivity method for statistical disclosure and applied statistics. The study does not establish priority over all literature or guarantee journal acceptance.

## Run a new audit

```python
from audit import Group, envelope, linear_certificate

anchor = (0.6, 0.4)
groups = (
    Group((0.8, 0.3), (0.60, 0.40), (0.66, 0.44)),
    Group((0.2, 0.7), (0.50, 0.32), (0.54, 0.36)),
)
exact = envelope(anchor, groups, rho=0.10)
print(exact.lower, exact.upper)
bracket = linear_certificate(anchor, groups, rho=0.10)
print(bracket.lower, bracket.inner_lower, bracket.inner_upper, bracket.upper)
```

Each group supplies its proportion at every site, then the lower and upper outcome rates. The two group proportions sum to one at each site. All endpoints use proportions; multiply by 100 for percentage points. A witness with indices i, j and fraction t replaces the designated share by a mix putting t on site i and the remainder on j. To reconstruct an attaining table choose the upper rate where the signed coefficient in Theorem 1 is positive and the lower rate elsewhere. For the lower endpoint negate the orientation. The real-arithmetic results are exact; the implementation uses double precision.

## Data and outputs

The two bundled ZIPs are the public NCES IPEDS admissions archives retrieved on 23 November 2026. The analysis uses the revised 2022 member and the sole 2023 member of the pinned ZIP. It reads the women and men application and admitted-count fields with reported-value flags. Public totals are fixed. Rounding and Laplace noise are applied by this experiment. IPEDS did not release these inputs through the simulated mechanisms. The source URL, member names and SHA-256 pins identify the supplied vintage.
